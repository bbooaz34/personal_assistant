Aura monitor Open the interactive viewer

Designing a 2.1-inch circle, and testing it on the device before it exists

Clearblue’s Aura is a connected fertility monitor with a small round display. We designed three directions for what that display shows, then built a 3D model and an interactive viewer so each direction could be judged on the object itself, with the test stick in and the result on screen.

Rendered in Blender Cycles from the same model the viewer loads.

Why build the device at all

A flat frame hides what a device screen really is: a circle cut out of a square, under glass, held in a hand, with a stick sliding in at the one moment that matters. Nine screens in Figma could tell us whether they were tidy. They could not tell us how each one reads inside the bezel, next to the others, in the order the user meets them.

So the brief for the model was narrow. It did not need to be a manufacturing file. It needed to be believable enough that a reviewer stops looking at artwork and starts looking at a product.

Building the model

1

Shape the body

A tapered ellipsoid on a 256 by 128 mesh. On a surface this smooth, low-resolution triangles showed up as facets, so the normals come from the ellipsoid’s own equation instead of being averaged from the mesh.

2

Cut the shell

Boolean-cut pieces: a pearl upper shell, a champagne band and a base cap, with a seam and a slot for the stick. Shell surfaces that overlapped flickered against each other until the recesses were cut into the upper shell itself.

3

Set the glass

A circular bezel with a cover glass over the display, which is a UV-mapped disc driven by an image. The screen emits its own light, so it glows behind the reflections the way a real LCD does.

4

Make the stick move

The test stick and its cap are separate parts on their own axis, so the viewer can bring the stick in with two moves: line it up, then slide it into the slot.

5

Bake it for the web

The working scene keeps every boolean and bevel live. For export, a clean copy is rebuilt from the evaluated geometry each time and named for the file, so nothing from the editing process leaks through. The result is 12 named parts in a 3.2 MB GLB.

6

Light it like a product shot

For the portfolio, the same GLB went back into Blender: a seamless studio sweep, a key, rim and fill, a thin strip for the glass, and a full turn rendered in Cycles, frame by frame.

The modelling was done in Blender, with Blender and Figma both connected to Claude through MCP, so geometry and screens could be adjusted from the same conversation.

A viewer you can send as a link

The viewer is a single HTML file built on three.js. The model, the lighting environment and all nine screens are inside it, so it opens from a link or a folder with nothing to install.

Turn and zoom

Drag to orbit the monitor, scroll or pinch to move in. Reset view brings it back to the front.

Pick a direction

Three tabs switch the whole experience, and a screen list changes what the display shows.

Start test

The stick slides in, the measuring moment plays, and the display lands on the result. New test takes the stick out again.

Believable glass

A studio lighting environment, with reflections set low so the cover reads as glass without hiding the screen.

It also keeps a light record of how it is used: which direction a reviewer opens and how long they stay, which screen they look at, and whether they turn or zoom the model. That turns a presentation into a small, honest piece of research about where attention goes.

What the viewer let us check

Does the circle hold?

Layouts that sit comfortably in a square get clipped by a bezel. Seeing them in the real crop showed which directions lean on rings and which reshape their panels to follow the curve.

Does each ground work?

Navy, white and pale blue sit in the same bezel under the same light, so contrast and the risk of backlight glow can be discussed against the object, not a swatch.

Is the moment right?

Start, wait, result is part of the design. The viewer plays that sequence in order, which a set of static frames cannot.

Does it feel related to the app?

Three directions side by side on one device make it clear how far each travels from the Period Tracker, and which familiar cues it keeps.
